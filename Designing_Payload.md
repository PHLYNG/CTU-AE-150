# Designing a Payload

Every successful aerospace mission begins not with soldering irons or raw code, but with a question: What is this mission trying to achieve? In aerospace and systems engineering, requirements drive design. You cannot select a microcontroller, choose a battery chemistry, or size a thermal enclosure until you know what the system is required to do and what constraints it must operate within.

## Requirements Drive Design

Before writing requirements, start by stepping back and examining your goals and physical boundaries:   
1. What is the Payload’s Objective? Ask yourself what primary questions your mission will answer. High-altitude balloons carry payloads to the edge of space, but the scientific or operational focus dictates every subsystem you choose:   Atmospheric Characterization: Are you measuring vertical temperature gradients, pressure decay, or humidity profiles as you ascend through the troposphere and stratosphere?   Visual Imagery: Are you capturing wide-angle video or high-resolution stills of the curvature of the Earth and the blackness of space?   Remote Sensing & Experiments: Are you testing solar panel degradation, radiation exposure, or custom RF transmitters?   Your scientific or engineering objective establishes your functional and performance requirements—defining exactly what tasks the hardware must execute and how accurately it must measure.
