# Introduction

## Why High Altitude Ballooning?

Getting to space is hard. But High-Altitude Ballooning (HAB) provides an authentic, accessible, and low-cost gateway to conduct real science experiments directly in a near-space environment. While CubeSats has made space more accessible than ever to students and offer invaluable flight experience, completing a CubeSat project can still take months or even years. In contrast, HAB provides rapid turnaround times, empowering student teams to design, build, test, and fly functional payloads within a single academic semester.

On a manageable scale, a HAB mission mirrors the complete project life cycle: originating a concept, sourcing widely available commercial components, rapid prototyping, bench-level integration, verification testing, and mission execution. Building a payload from end to end naturally sits at the convergence of multiple engineering disciplines:

  1. Mechanical & Structural Design: Sizing lightweight enclosures, fabricating custom brackets, and implementing thermal protection to survive the harsh stratospheric environment down to -60°C and parachute landing shocks.
  2. Electrical Engineering: Integrating microcontrollers (e.g., ESP32), power distribution, and sensor arrays.
  3. Software Development & Sensor Integration: Writing custom code to interface multi-sensor suites over digital buses (e.g. I2C, UART), synchronizing data streams, and ensuring reliable data logging.
  4. Data Analysis & Post-Flight Evaluation: Parsing recovered flight records to evaluate physical atmospheric phenomena and quantify actual system performance.

Most importantly, HAB payloads demand the very same disciplined systems engineering techniques used by NASA to deliver successful flight missions. Navigating regulatory mass limits, hardware integration, and thermal survival requires rigorous requirements traceability, clear interface definitions, and structured flight-readiness reviews.

Beyond technical execution, ballooning serves as a bridge to the wider aerospace community. At Capitol Technology University (CTU), HAB has actively fostered inter-institutional collaboration with regional partners like the University of Maryland and Howard University. It transforms theoretical aerospace concepts into a practical, multi-disciplinary, and team driven launchpad for future engineers.
