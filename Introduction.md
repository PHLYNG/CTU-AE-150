# Introduction

## Why High Altitude Ballooning?

Accessing space is notoriously resource-intensive, but High-Altitude Ballooning (HAB) provides an authentic, accessible, and low-cost gateway to conduct real science experiments directly in a near-space environment. While orbital platforms like CubeSats offer invaluable flight heritage, their launch queues and rigid manifest cycles can take months or even years. In contrast, HAB provides rapid turnaround times, empowering student teams to design, build, test, and fly functional payloads within a single academic semester.

On a manageable scale, a HAB mission mirrors the complete project life cycle: originating a concept, sourcing commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) components, rapid prototyping, bench-level integration, verification testing, and mission execution. Building a payload from end to end naturally sits at the convergence of multiple engineering disciplines:
Mechanical & Structural Design: Sizing lightweight enclosures, fabricating custom brackets, and implementing thermal protection to survive the harsh stratospheric environment down to -60C and parachute landing shocks.
Electrical & Firmware Engineering: Integrating microcontrollers (e.g., ESP32), power distribution networks, and telemetry tracking systems.

Software Development & Sensor Integration: Writing custom code to interface multi-sensor suites over digital buses (e.g. I2C, UART), synchronizing data streams, and ensuring reliable data logging.

Data Analysis & Post-Flight Evaluation: Parsing recovered flight records to evaluate physical atmospheric phenomena and quantify actual system performance.

Most importantly, HAB payloads demand the very same disciplined systems engineering techniques used by NASA to deliver successful flight missions. Navigating regulatory mass limits (under 6 lbs), hardware integration, and thermal survival requires rigorous requirements traceability, clear interface definitions, and structured flight-readiness reviews.

Beyond technical execution, ballooning serves as a bridge to the wider aerospace community. At Capitol Technology University (CTU), HAB has actively fostered inter-institutional collaboration with regional partners like the University of Maryland and Howard University. It transforms theoretical aerospace concepts into a practical, multi-disciplinary launchpad for future engineers.
