# CTU-Senior-Design

High Altitude Balloon Calibration, Integration, and Testing Guide

Capitol Technology University (CTU) currently lacks a standardized and formally documented Calibration, Integration, and Testing (CINT) framework for High-Altitude Balloon (HAB) payload development. Although HAB missions have historically been conducted in collaboration with other organizations, CTU does not maintain an internally controlled and repeatable procedural baseline governing payload calibration, subsystem integration, environmental verification, or flight readiness review. As a result, technical consistency across student cohorts depends largely on instructor guidance and informal knowledge transfer rather than documented systems engineering processes.

The NASA Systems Engineering Handbook emphasizes that disciplined integration and verification practices are foundational to mission reliability and lifecycle success (NASA, 2016). Likewise, the International Council on Systems Engineering (INCOSE) identifies configuration control, verification traceability, and documented validation procedures as essential characteristics of mature engineering organizations (INCOSE, 2015). In the absence of these structured processes, CTU’s HAB capability remains vulnerable to integration inconsistencies, preventable technical errors, and the loss of institutional knowledge when students graduate. This situation limits instructional scalability in AE-150: Introduction to Space and reduces the long-term sustainability of CTU’s independent HAB program.

The proposed solution is the development of a comprehensive, CTU-specific CINT technical manual. This guide will establish standardized procedures governing payload calibration protocols, subsystem integration workflows, environmental stress testing, flight readiness verification, and post-flight data validation. Rather than adopting a generalized framework, the guide will be intentionally tailored to CTU’s Fusion Lab resources, available instrumentation, faculty expertise, and the pedagogical requirements of freshman-level engineering students.
